GREU → EU Framework

Data roadmap & progress tracker — replacing Statistics Denmark with EU-wide sources
Status: 17 August 2026
DREAM · GREU

Goal: a version of GREU that any EU member state can run, using only data available for all EU countries — primarily Eurostat and JRC FIGARO — while staying as close as possible to the Danish model's structure and detail. The Danish input files define the target; the work is refilling them from EU sources. New to this? §§1–2 explain the model and specialist terms; §4 maps each input to an EU source; §6 gives the verified pilot evidence.

What has been proven so far: five Denmark-2020 pilots tested the economic, physical-energy, emissions, energy-purpose, and monetary-energy layers. FIGARO reproduces main national-account totals to within 0.1%; Eurostat PEFA reproduces comparable physical energy to within 0.61%; and JRC-IDEES reproduces the combined industrial process envelope to within 3.05%. The public Union Registry then reproduces GREU's ETS totals within 0.007% and reveals how its “bought allowances” were derived. The fifth pilot shows that public monetary sources are controls, not a direct replacement: 0% of nonzero GREU monetary energy cells has every required component observed at the necessary grain. The approved calibrated method has now produced the first non-Danish package: Sweden 2020 public_core passes its workbook/GDX contract without Danish values or shares. It balances 4,611.0794 PJ and 610.583 bn SEK, while visibly retaining 1,765.088 PJ of reporting-detail residual and 118.844 bn SEK of unmatched broad SUT controls (narrowed from 299.131 bn SEK on 31 July, plus a newly-disclosed 102.567 bn SEK supply-side residual — see §6). Two same-day evidence pilots (PRODCOM and waste statistics, 31 July) then showed the two largest residual pieces are overwhelmingly non-energy money — real spending on non-energy goods and services sharing a broad category with energy, not hidden energy value (§6). What remains is chiefly classification and construction: exact industry-purpose energy in PJ (including in_ETS), narrower product controls, the investment split, and conversion of the model's other still-Danish inputs. That conversion has now started: a sixth Denmark pilot (31 July) tested the smallest remaining input — employment — and Eurostat reproduces its load-bearing content essentially exactly (hours match to the rounding digit; see §6), confirming the first “OK” verdict of the new one-input-per-increment parameterization phase.
Sweden package accepted · compatibility boundary only The monetary-energy interface will use a coarser public-EU core with an optional detailed country layer. In plain language, every country gets the same public-data ceiling; a country may add better national detail, but that detail must be explicit, validated and reproducible. Denmark remains the default detailed layer, so the current run is unchanged. A public-core run fails if its generated country package is missing—there is no hidden fallback to Danish files or shares. Sweden's package validates workbook sheets, columns, unique keys and a complete marginal-rate GDX. Monetary cells are clearly labelled calibrated/modelled: 0 are direct and 117 nonzero use cells are modelled. This is not a full Sweden model run; unrelated IO, employment, assets, government, emissions and other inputs in the current preprocessing remain Danish.

Verified means downloaded and reconciled numerically. Open means a decision, construction method, or source/vintage explanation is still needed. Current status and verdicts live in docs/eu_data_mapping.md; pilot evidence and every reconciliation number in docs/eu_data_pilots.md and the linked workbooks.

1Orientationread this first if the files are new to you

GREU is a GAMS model of the Danish economy and its energy use / emissions. It never reads Excel directly: a Python preprocessing step turns the input workbooks into data.gdx, and the model reads only that file. Everything in this roadmap is about the left-hand end of that chain.

  (DK detail by default; public core fails closed)                                      ├──► data_from_GR.gms ──► data.gdx ──► GREU
  Eurostat, live ──► financial_accounts_data.py ──► fin_acc.gdx ────────────────────────┘      same symbols + tests

Traced end-to-end 28 Jul. data_from_GR.gms is the natural integration point for EU data. Implemented 30 Jul: only the two monetary-energy workbook paths, the complete marginal-rate GDX and generated country GDX are configurable; all unrelated transformations remain unchanged. The financial-accounts branch already pulls Eurostat live (PR #107) — it is out of scope as a work stream but is the in-repo template every replacement should copy.

2Vocabularythe acronyms used throughout

NACE / A64The EU industry classification. A64 is the 64-industry breakdown Eurostat publishes — the effective ceiling on industry detail. GREU has 57 industries, some of which split a single A64 code further.
CPAThe product classification paired with NACE (what is produced, rather than who produces it).
COICOPClassification of household consumption by purpose. GREU's 12 consumption groups map onto it.
ESA 2010The EU national-accounts standard. Its transaction codes appear everywhere: P3 consumption, P51g investment, D1 compensation of employees, D21 product taxes.
SUT / IOTSupply-and-use tables / input-output table — who buys what from whom. GREU's IOT is industry-by-industry.
FIGAROEurostat/JRC inter-country input-output tables: every EU country on one consistent basis, 2010–2024, 64×64, all members + 18 partners + RoW. The backbone of the EU version.
PEFAPhysical Energy Flow Accounts — energy in physical units (PJ) by industry and product. The EU counterpart to the physical side of the Danish energy data.
Basic → purchaser priceThe monetary “price ladder”. GREU starts from producer/basic value, adds three trade margins, five named energy taxes and VAT, and reaches what the user pays. The model sums those components itself; a final retail price alone is not enough.
Residence principleEnergy/emission accounts count resident operators wherever they are (so Danish ships abroad are in), unlike territorial UNFCCC inventories. The difference is the "bridge items".
EUTL / Union RegistryThe European Commission's public registry of EU ETS installations, verified emissions, free allocation and surrendered units. It publishes anonymous daily bulk files. Its activity codes describe regulated processes; they are not NACE industry codes.
JRC-IDEESJRC's open analytical energy end-use database. The current JRC-IDEES-2023 v1 covers every EU member state annually for 2000–2023. Its detailed processes are estimated while constrained to Eurostat energy-balance totals.
SDMXThe API through which Eurostat serves data; how our download scripts fetch it.
.gdxGAMS's binary data format — the handover format between the Python preprocessing and the model.
GFCFGross Fixed Capital Formation — the national-accounts term for investment spending (buying buildings, machinery, vehicles, etc. that will be used for years). It is the input needed to build GREU's investment split (§5③).
RASA standard matrix-balancing technique (named after its inventors) that adjusts a whole matrix of numbers just enough to match new row and column totals, while disturbing the original pattern as little as possible. Used to fit a starting matrix (e.g. Denmark's) to a different country's published totals.
DG TAXUDThe European Commission's Directorate-General for Taxation and Customs Union — the official source for EU VAT and excise-duty rate tables used to check Sweden's VAT rate.
CPA product codes
(e.g. CPA_B05, CPA_C16, CPA_E37-E39)		Codes from the EU's product classification (paired with NACE, see above). CPA_B05/B06 = coal and crude-oil extraction; CPA_C16 = wood products; CPA_E37-E39 = sewerage/waste/remediation services. These specific codes matter because Sweden's national accounts group energy products together with unrelated ones under them — see §5① and §7 Q7.

3What we start from

The team has already built concordances in metadata.xlsx: GREU's 57 industries → NACE A64, 12 consumption groups → 4-digit COICOP, 25 energy products → PEFA codes, flows/sectors/assets → ESA-2010. The target structure is thus already expressed in EU vocabulary — the work is sourcing EU data on the right-hand side of each map, not inventing the maps. The Danish energy data semantics are separately verified and documented in data/preprocessing/data/energy_data_notes.md, and the official GREU data documentation.pdf (9 pp., in the data folder) describes the Danish inputs as built — its §1.2.3 itself lists the Eurostat databases publishing the same aggregates, which is an endorsement of the route this roadmap takes. Scope: the contents of data/preprocessing/data/ only. Adjacent streams — the technology catalogue and the financial-accounts module — are out of scope.

5The four structural gapswhere the real work is

① Energy IO in money

Denmark gets monetary energy accounts from DST; no EU equivalent. The audit found 0% direct cell coverage: SUT, tax accounts and retail prices constrain different margins and never meet at product×user×purpose. Sweden implementation: the approved coarse calibration now passes, and its unmatched monetary residual was narrowed from 299.131 to 118.844 bn SEK (31 Jul) by fixing a PEFA reporting-granularity mismatch — its 1,765.088 PJ physical-detail residual and remaining 118.844 (use) + 102.567 (supply) bn SEK residuals now reflect genuinely non-energy CPA scope, not a data gap. Two 31 July evidence pilots confirm this: PRODCOM shows the wood-products piece is ≥98.5% non-energy, and waste statistics bound the waste/sewerage piece at ≥85% non-energy (§6) — closing the residual work stream as a data question, pending colleague acceptance.

② Purpose dimension

IDEES supplies detailed industrial processes, but not GREU's five labels. Heating is only a proxy; normal/special require a concordance; transport cannot be assigned to user industries. EUTL identifies ETS installations and emissions but publishes no fuel or PJ. PEFA already supplies broad household totals. Route: IDEES process rules + PEFA controls + EUTL membership + a reviewed industry bridge and fuel/emission-factor model for in_ETS.

③ Investment split

Denmark can see how much of each industry's investment is buildings, vehicles or machinery, and who supplies those goods. EU data cannot. Rescoped 7 Aug: a code audit showed the model never uses the full matrix, only two simpler breakdowns (§6) — so this gap is smaller than previously recorded. Route: the supply side is largely a classification job; the use side is nearly direct data for 13/27 countries (17 Aug probe: nama_10_a64_p5 publishes investment by asset at near-full industry detail there, incl. DK and SE); the other 14 countries mean splitting 21 industry groups into GREU's 57, using Denmark's pattern as a starting point. First step is a Denmark back-test against its own known answer.

④ Marginal tax rates

Imported from Danish GreenREFORM via EU_GR_data.gdx — a stopgap by the code's own admission. Route: derive from the gap-① excise/ETS layer, or redesign so average rates suffice.

Cross-cutting issue: industry splits finer than NACE A64 (organic/conventional farming, five waste industries, electricity 35011/35002) need country-specific splitting keys, or a decision to run other EU countries at the aggregated level — see Q3.

6Evidence so far — Denmark pilots and Sweden packageverified, not assumed

✓ Pilot 1 · The economic backbone works (FIGARO, 29 July)

FIGARO DK 2020 (2026 edition, industry-by-industry, pulled via the Eurostat SDMX API and converted at 7.4542 DKK/EUR) was reconciled cell-by-cell against the Danish io_long_format.xlsx. Headline aggregates match to ≤0.1% — total output 4055.4 bn DKK, compensation of employees 1210.4, gross surplus 824.0, government consumption 576.1, GFCF 516.1 — and GDP lands within +0.1% of the official Danish figure. Household-consumption identities reproduce exactly once FIGARO's adjustment rows are used. This is expected: FIGARO is benchmarked on the same national accounts. It means the EU route reproduces Denmark, which is the premise the whole project rests on.

Known differences, all explainable: ① Danish re-exports (239.8 bn) have no equivalent in FIGARO's inter-country logic, so headline imports differ by −22% (−2.3% once re-exports are excluded); ② import composition differs because FIGARO carries foreign margins as separate service rows where Danish CIF goods imports embed them; ③ the NACE-L ↔ GREU 68203 real-estate mapping is not value-consistent (±95 bn) and needs a split key; ④ granularity is coarser in 7 of 28 mapping clusters, covering 34 of 57 GREU industries. (Cluster count corrected 31 Jul: the concordance's own artifact contains 28 mapped clusters, not the 24 quoted in earlier prose; the 7 coarser clusters and industry coverage are unaffected.) Full detail and re-runnable scripts: docs/eu_data_pilots.md, figaro_dk2020_reconciliation.xlsx.

✓ First non-Danish package · Sweden 2020 monetary-energy public core (30 July)

Why Sweden: it has complete 2020 PEFA, SUT, environmental-tax and electricity/gas price observations, a non-euro currency that tests exchange-rate handling, and a different energy structure from Denmark. It is therefore a useful first proof that the interface is country-generic rather than a disguised Danish reconstruction.

What “calibrated allocation” means: PEFA observes physical quantities; SUT observes broad CPA totals; price series observe selected product families. The builder uses those public observations as controls and explicit weights, then scales modelled product/user cells to the published totals. It never treats those allocated cells as source observations and never uses Danish monetary or purpose shares.

Result: supply and use both equal 4,611.0794 PJ; purchaser value is 610.583 bn SEK. Maximum product, component and SUT purchaser residuals are numerical noise (≤2.84×10⁻14 in their respective units). The direct/modelled distinction is decisive: 0 monetary cells are direct; 117 nonzero use cells are modelled. Sweden's PEFA reporting leaves 1,765.088 PJ above the safe A64 allocation ceiling, and broad SUT controls leave 118.844 bn SEK without matched physical quantity on the use side, plus a separately-tracked 102.567 bn SEK on the supply/producer side. Both appear as explicit residuals. Negative inventory values are retained and flagged.

Compatibility verdict: PASS for public_core + SE: both workbooks and the complete GDX validate, deterministic table/GDX records reproduce, and 10 focused tests pass. Air-account totals are not allocated because they mix energy and process emissions. The full GREU model is intentionally not run: its non-energy inputs are still Danish.

Found and fixed by a 31 July verification pass: the builder could not originally tell "the source publishes a real zero" apart from "the source publishes no row at all" — both looked identical (0.0) and neither raised a warning. This hid a real gap: Eurostat has no coal or crude-oil SUT breakdown for Sweden at all, so 916.7847 PJ (≈20% of Sweden's energy) had a silently unflagged monetary value. It now raises an explicit anomaly instead. No other number on this page changed — the fix only turns a hidden gap into a disclosed one.

Unmatched SUT residual narrowed, same day (31 July): Sweden's PEFA reports energy use for whole NACE sections (manufacturing, agriculture, water/waste, trade/transport) without ever breaking them into the finer divisions the GREU concordance expects, while the national accounts publish their money at that finer level. The energy was never missing — it was already sitting in the explicit indu=res reporting-detail residual — its money was the piece wrongly booked as unmatched. Pooling that money into the same bucket as its physical counterpart cut the use-side residual from 299.131 to 118.844 bn SEK (180.287 bn SEK reclassified, ~60%), with every identity still closing exactly. The same investigation also found the headline metric never looked at the supply side at all (it only reads a purchaser-price field that supply rows never populate), exposing a comparable 102.567 bn SEK producer-side residual that is now disclosed rather than invisible. Industries Sweden's statistics do detail (e.g. financial services) were never touched — what remains for them is genuinely non-energy spending inside a too-broad product category, which is a different, harder problem than a reporting gap.

✓ Residual characterization · The two big residuals are non-energy money (PRODCOM + waste statistics, 31 July)

The question: after the narrowing above, Sweden's package still carries money in two broad official categories with no matching physical energy — 42.763 bn SEK of wood products (CPA_C16, 40.665 bn of it the export control) and 60.156 bn SEK of waste/sewerage services (CPA_E37-E39, supply side). Is that hidden energy money, or genuinely non-energy spending that merely shares a broad category with energy products? Two parallel read-only pilots answered with public EU-wide data.

PRODCOM pilot: PRODCOM is Eurostat's product-level manufacturing statistic — sold-production and export values per detailed (8-digit) product code, far finer than the SUT's broad categories. It shows only ~1.15% of Sweden's wood-products (NACE division 16) export value is energy-relevant products (wood chips, pellets/briquettes) — so only ~0.5–0.6 bn SEK of the 42.763 bn SEK residual is plausibly energy money. PRODCOM's division-16 exports reconcile with the SUT export control (ratio 0.979), so it is a viable EU-wide splitting key for this category. Access correction: the dataset code previously cited in the project notes (prc_stapro) does not exist; the working dataset is DS-059358 on the Eurostat Comext dissemination API, with all EU-27 present. Caveat: pellet production value is confidential in 5 of 27 countries (including Sweden itself), so export-based shares are the robust route.

Waste-statistics pilot: at most 1.7%–14.8% (central estimate 1.0 bn SEK) of the 60.156 bn SEK waste/sewerage control is plausibly energy-relevant. The key insight resolves an apparent paradox: Sweden burns 50% of its non-mineral waste for energy (78.3 PJ), yet 100% of that fuel is burned in power/heat plants classified in the energy sector (NACE D35) — so the energy revenue never appears in the waste sector's accounts. The waste sector's money is collection fees, gate fees, materials-recovery trading and sewerage services. Sources: SBS (sbs_na_ind_r2), EPEA (env_ac_pepsgg1/env_ac_pepssp1), EGSS (env_ac_egss2), env_wastrt and nrg_bal_c, each covering 25–27 of the EU-27. None enables a product-level split, so this residual is characterizable but not narrowable with public EU-wide data.

Consequence: both residuals are now evidence-backed as overwhelmingly non-energy money. The recommendation to colleagues is to accept them as permanent, disclosed features of the coarse public-core method — that acceptance is the pending decision (§7 Q7), now supported by evidence instead of assertion. The Sweden package itself was not rebuilt: these pilots are read-only evidence and change no runtime artifact, code or test. Detailed workbooks: prodcom_se2020_c16_reconciliation.xlsx, waste_stats_se2020_e37e39_feasibility.xlsx; re-runnable scripts in data/preprocessing/scripts/, raw deliveries with provenance under prodcom_raw/SE/2020/ and waste_stats_raw/SE/2020/.

✓ Pilot 2 · The physical energy backbone works (PEFA + air accounts, 30 July)

PEFA is Eurostat's physical ledger of energy entering, moving through, and leaving the economy. On a like-for-like boundary, Eurostat reports 2,237.794 PJ versus GREU's 2,251.550 PJ: −13.756 PJ (−0.611%). Both datasets balance supply and use. Household energy is almost exact: 253.760 versus 253.662 PJ (+0.039%); exports and inventories are also within 0.1%. The raw PEFA headline of 3,702.820 PJ is not comparable because it also counts upstream natural inputs and energy losses/dissipative heat.

What detail is lost: Eurostat stops at NACE A64 (coarser in 7 mapping clusters covering 36/57 GREU industries), has no industry split into GREU's six energy purposes, and merges the three explicit bunker fuels into ordinary fuel products. It does retain broad household heating/transport/other uses. The pilot also found four fixable concordance issues: missing P18 gasoil, misclassified ambient heat, a semi_refin_oil spelling mismatch, and 0.555 PJ of unmapped derived gas.

Air emissions: Eurostat publishes total energy + non-energy emissions, so both GREU emissions workbooks must be combined. Fossil CO₂ then matches within −0.0069% and F-gases within 0.001 kt CO₂e. Total GHG is +1.111% in Eurostat (82.241 vs 81.337 Mt CO₂e), driven mainly by +26.430 kt CH₄ and +0.636 kt N₂O, especially agriculture. This is an open source/vintage difference, not rounding.

Why it matters: EU data can reproduce the physical totals needed for modelling, while making the remaining construction problems explicit. Detailed workbook: eurostat_energy_emissions_dk2020_reconciliation.xlsx; methods and audit: docs/eu_data_pilots.md.

✓ Pilot 3 · IDEES narrows—but does not close—the purpose gap (30 July)

What IDEES is: JRC-IDEES-2023 v1 is an official, open (CC BY 4.0) analytical database for all EU-27 countries, annual 2000–2023. Country ZIP files download directly without login. It starts from Eurostat energy-balance totals and estimates a detailed split into 11 industrial sectors, 21 subsectors, named processes, cross-cutting uses, and energy carriers. This makes it reproducible and EU-generic, but the fine end uses are modelled estimates, not separately observed statistics.

Boundary test: for the industries that can be mapped safely, IDEES reports 96.610 PJ versus GREU 129.636 PJ. The headline −25.48% is misleading because GREU assigns 20.999 PJ of resident firms' own-account transport to user industries, while IDEES follows territorial energy-balance sectors. Excluding that transport gives the closer comparison: 96.610 versus 108.637 PJ (−11.07%).

Purpose result: the combined industrial process envelope is strong: IDEES proxies total 93.116 PJ versus GREU process_normal + process_special + in_ETS of 90.360 PJ (+3.05%). The exact labels do not transfer directly. IDEES low-enthalpy heat is only 3.494 PJ versus GREU heating 18.277 PJ; the pilot special-process proxy is 26.489 PJ versus GREU 5.764 PJ, so an owner-approved process concordance is essential. IDEES cannot allocate own-account transport to user industries and has no ETS-status field. The next pilot tested EUTL directly.

Households: PEFA remains the account control. IDEES adds richer residential detail and matches GREU's non-transport household subtotal closely: 179.040 versus 179.463 PJ (−0.236%). IDEES residential plus PEFA transport gives 253.597 PJ, only −0.025% from GREU, but this must be documented as a hybrid construction. Detailed workbook: jrc_idees_dk2020_purpose_reconciliation.xlsx.

✓ Pilot 4 · Public Union Registry reproduces ETS totals—but not in_ETS energy (30 July)

Access: the European Commission Union Registry publishes anonymous daily GZIP-CSV files for the operator master and annual installation compliance records. The 30 July 2026 snapshot contains all EU-27 countries and years 2005–2025. The EEA July 2026 release adds documented aggregates and national auction volumes under CC BY 4.0. Raw files, hashes, URLs and scripts are preserved.

Denmark 2020: current public data report verified emissions 11,039.385 versus GREU 11,038.641 kt CO₂e (+0.0067%); free allocation 6,783.620 versus 6,783.462 thousand (+0.0023%). GREU's bought_allowances is not an observed purchase: it reproduces as the installation-level positive shortfall max(emissions − free allocation, 0), 5,348.399 versus 5,348.293 thousand (+0.0020%). GREU's 1.239901 bn DKK cost then implies a uniform 231.831 DKK/t price; the Registry itself has no price or tax field. EEA's 6,845.500 thousand auction volume is a country total and is not the same concept.

Industry bridge: EUTL activity codes are regulatory activities, not NACE. A current public secondary concordance derived from 2015/2020 carbon-leakage lists covers 356/375 DK records and 97.59% of emissions, but is historical, partial and non-authoritative. It is suitable for a diagnostic A64/GREU cluster comparison, not an automatic production mapping.

Why in_ETS stays open: EUTL reports emissions and allowances, not PJ, fuels, heat content or GREU purposes. Constructing ETS-covered energy requires a maintained installation→industry map, fuel-specific emission factors, separation of process emissions, PEFA/IDEES controls and model rules preventing double counting. Detailed workbook: eutl_dk2020_reconciliation.xlsx.

✓ Pilot 5 · Public monetary sources constrain—but cannot directly build—the energy price layer (30 July)

Why this layer matters: physical PJ says how much energy moves; the monetary layer says who pays how much and separates producer value from wholesale, retail and motor-vehicle margins, five Danish-style energy taxes, VAT and purchaser value. GREU sums these components itself and checks them against a separate energy-only IO table. A single average retail price therefore does not satisfy the model interface.

What was tested: Eurostat national supply/use valuation tables, environmental taxes by payer, annual electricity/gas price components, the Commission Weekly Oil Bulletin, and TAXUD excise/VAT references. All are anonymously accessible. Electricity components and environmental taxes have EU-27 2020 coverage; gas is 24/27 for households and 25/27 for non-households. National SUT is 26/27 because Bulgaria is absent; none of those missing combinations appears in a 2015–2024 live probe.

Denmark result: PEFA still directly covers 2,237.794 / 2,251.550 PJ (99.389%) on the comparable physical boundary, but 0 of 862 nonzero monetary use rows (0%) has all required valuation components directly observed at product×user×purpose grain. Exact-family controls for electricity, road petrol and road diesel cover 337.775 PJ (15.002%); adding mixed natural gas/biogas as a near-match reaches 481.430 PJ (21.382%). These are control envelopes, not populated cells.

What is available versus missing: SUT supplies broad CPA basic and purchaser totals, one combined trade/transport margin and net product taxes; tax accounts supply payer totals; retail data supply selected product/band averages. Missing are the cross-classified cell, the three GREU margins, five-tax split, and VAT incidence. The Danish target identities and energy-IO links were revalidated within source rounding, but no Danish shares were used to fill public-data gaps.

Readiness verdict at the feasibility stage: no direct fill was possible without modelling choices. The owner subsequently approved the transparent calibrated engine used by the Sweden package above. That implementation resolves interface feasibility but does not change the 0% direct-cell finding or remove the need to retain residuals. Detailed evidence: energy_money_dk2020_feasibility_gap.xlsx.

✓ Pilot 6 · Employment reconciles essentially exactly — the parameterization phase opens (nama_10_a64_e, 31 July)

A new phase: with the monetary-energy core proven, the work has moved to converting the model's remaining Danish inputs to EU sources, one input per increment, smallest first. The first is employed.xlsx — employment and hours worked per industry, split into employees and self-employed. Before comparing anything, the pilot inspected what the model actually consumes, and the answer is surprisingly little: per industry it uses only the ratio of hours worked by the self-employed to hours worked by employees (to impute a labour income for the self-employed, who do not pay themselves a recorded wage), plus one national total head count. Per-industry head counts are never used individually.

Denmark 2020 result: on the load-bearing hours content, Eurostat's nama_10_a64_e reproduces the Danish file essentially exactly — national totals identical to the rounding digit (3,982,709 thousand hours on both sides), employee and self-employed totals also exact, and 24 of 28 industry clusters exact. Person head counts differ by a uniform +3.52%: a definitional difference in who counts as an "employed person", not a data error — the exact hours match proves both sides rest on the same underlying national accounts. It affects only the single national scalar, and is now a question for the Danish colleagues (Q8). One known problem resurfaced for the third time: GREU industry 68203 covers dwellings only, but the concordance maps all of NACE section L (real estate) to it, producing a +180% difference in that cluster. The split-key decision (Phase 1 task) is still open and now also blocks employment.

EU-wide coverage: 16 of 27 member states publish everything needed at the full 64-industry detail. Germany, France, Belgium, Bulgaria and Lithuania publish hours only at a coarser industry level (Estonia coarser still); the documented workaround — distributing aggregate hours across industries by each industry's share of persons — is mild, because only the self-employed/employee hours ratio enters the model. Sweden suppresses 6 of 63 industry codes, but published parent aggregates allow partial recovery. Where countries suppress the self-employed figure (HU, FI, LT, EE), it is recoverable as total employment minus employees. Verdict: the mapping table's "OK" is confirmed. Artifacts: download_employment_dk_2020.py and reconcile_employment_dk_2020.py in data/preprocessing/scripts/, raw deliveries with provenance under employment_raw/DK/2020/, workbook employment_dk2020_reconciliation.xlsx.

✓ Pilot 7 · The emissions bridge reconciles with zero coverage gaps (env_ac_aibrid_r2, 17 August)

What this input does: national emission statistics exist in two flavours. The residence principle counts everything caused by a country's residents wherever it happens (a Danish ship burning fuel off Singapore counts for Denmark); the territory principle of the UN climate inventories counts everything happening on national soil, whoever causes it. GREU works with residence-based accounts but must also report territory-based UNFCCC totals, so it needs the small "bridge" between the two — border fuel purchases, international transport — plus the LULUCF term (emissions and removals from land use and forests, which only the inventory carries). The Danish input file holds exactly these bridge items.

Denmark 2020 result: Eurostat publishes the identical concept as env_ac_aibrid_r2, and a single dataset covers all three rows of the Danish file, LULUCF included. The total residence adjustment matches to within 0.05% per gas (fossil CO2: 38,482 vs 38,484 thousand tonnes, −0.006%). Denmark's split of that total into two rows follows a national convention — international road hauliers sit under "international transport" in the Danish file but under "land transport" at Eurostat — and the pilot quantified this as a pure internal reshuffle of ~362 kt CO2 whose two halves cancel. Since only the border-trade row and the LULUCF total ever reach the model, this costs nothing: border trade can be built from Eurostat's net land-transport item. The LULUCF level differs by +17.7% for a known, benign reason: the Danish file predates recent revisions of the UN inventory, which routinely restates LULUCF heavily — the concept matches cell-for-cell against the current inventory (verified via env_air_gge). Both sides use the same global-warming-potential factors (verified arithmetically). All 19 internal consistency checks pass.

EU-wide coverage: complete. All 27 member states publish every needed cell for 2020 — the first pilot without a single coverage gap. Verdict: the mapping table's "OK" is confirmed. The same vintage effect just seen on LULUCF is expected for the next pilot (government finances, whose Danish values come from the MAKRO model rather than raw statistics). Artifacts: download_emissions_bridge_dk_2020.py and reconcile_emissions_bridge_dk_2020.py in data/preprocessing/scripts/, raw deliveries with provenance under emissions_bridge_raw/DK/2020/, workbook emissions_bridge_dk2020_reconciliation.xlsx.

✓ Pilot 8 · Government finances reconcile to the øre (gov_10a_main + gov_10a_taxag, 17 August)

What this input does: the model needs the government's budget by type of transaction — consumption, investment, subsidies, transfers, interest, and each family of taxes (VAT, income taxes, excises, ...) — as national scalar series. The Danish file carries 34 such rows for 2020, and the project had flagged it with a warning: its values come from Denmark's MAKRO model rather than raw statistics, so the expectation was that Eurostat would match the concepts but not the numbers (as just seen with the LULUCF vintage gap).

Denmark 2020 result: the warning did not materialize. Every row that maps onto a Eurostat item reconciles exactly, to the third decimal of a billion DKK — public consumption 576.099 bn, investment 82.871 bn, VAT 231.650 bn, and so on through the total expenditure (1,220.039 bn) and revenue (1,228.387 bn) sums. The single exception is interest revenue (+0.088 bn, +0.62%), which alone explains the small gap in the budget balance. Getting there required four documented re-readings, each verified numerically: Denmark's pension-yield tax (PAL, ~48 bn) sits inside Eurostat's household income taxes, not the corporate ones; the Danish "other product taxes" row silently includes import excises; dividends and rent are only published as one bundle for Denmark; and one Danish sheet stores components with signs. Two Danish label typos were found along the way. What remains is structural, small and enumerable: the split of transfers between domestic recipients and abroad, the dividends/rent detail, PAL as a separate series, and the EU-paid farm subsidies — each with a named candidate source (mostly the sector accounts dataset nasa_10_nf_tr) or a fixed-share fallback.

EU-wide coverage: 14 of 27 member states publish every item the mapping needs for 2020; the gaps elsewhere are mostly items that are plausibly just zero and unpublished (Eurostat drops zero cells), plus the patchy counterpart memo items already counted under the structural leftovers. Expenditure series run 1995–2025 for all 27. Verdict: the mapping table's "OK" is confirmed — and upgraded to number-exact. The transaction-code concordance was seeded from a colleague's Eurostat-only reference implementation (see the contribution note below). Artifacts: download_government_finances_dk_2020.py and reconcile_government_finances_dk_2020.py in data/preprocessing/scripts/, raw deliveries with provenance under government_finances_raw/DK/2020/, workbook government_finances_dk2020_reconciliation.xlsx.

A colleague independently built a stripped-down GREU variant that runs entirely on live Eurostat data — no energy, emissions or climate content, and industries aggregated to 19 sections instead of GREU's 57. It is committed as reference material under data/read_eurostat_data/ (see its README for provenance and caveats). ✓ Scope audit · The investment gap is smaller than recorded (code verification, 7 August)

What was assumed: Denmark's statistics office publishes a detailed investment table — for every pair of industries, how much investment equipment one sells to the other, split into buildings, vehicles and other machinery. No EU country publishes anything like it, and the project had recorded this whole table as something that would have to be rebuilt from scratch.

What the audit found: the model never reads that table. The preprocessing code discards the "who supplied it" dimension immediately — an existing comment in read_data.py says so in as many words — and keeps only how much of each type of capital each industry buys. The supply side re-enters much later, through the ordinary input-output machinery, using the investment columns that are already part of the main table. So the model needs two simple breakdowns, not one big matrix.

Why this matters: the two breakdowns are in much better shape than the matrix was. The supply side — which industries make investment goods — is essentially a classification exercise, since construction firms supply buildings and vehicle manufacturers supply vehicles; FIGARO already publishes the total to within 0.1% of the Danish figure. Only the use side is genuinely missing detail: Eurostat publishes it for 21 industry groups where GREU needs 57.

The catch, and the honest limit: the original idea was that using several years of data would let the finer split be estimated rather than assumed. Working through the arithmetic shows this works per industry group, and roughly needs as many years of data as there are GREU industries inside that group. Small groups will be pinned down by the data; large ones — manufacturing above all — will not, and there the Danish pattern will still be doing the work. That is a limitation to disclose, not to hide, and the estimator should say group by group which is which. Recommended first step: a Denmark back-test — hide Denmark's detailed table, rebuild it from only the coarse figures an ordinary member state would have, and see how close the reconstruction gets. It needs no new data and it tests the whole method, including whether Denmark's own pattern is stable enough over time to serve as anyone else's starting point. Full technical record, including the code references and the identification arithmetic, is in docs/eu_data_mapping.md.

Verified scope Both GREU and Eurostat environmental accounts use the residence principle: Danish-resident ships and aircraft count even when operating abroad. GREU's bunker products are 497.799 PJ / 39.138 Mt CO₂e; the official international-transport bridge is 39.022 Mt. Use that bridge to reach territorial/UNFCCC scope—do not subtract bunker rows ad hoc.
